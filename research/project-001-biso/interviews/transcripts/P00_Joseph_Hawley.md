Interviewer

Tell me about a situation where the business and cybersecurity genuinely disagreed about what the right outcome should be.

Walk me through the situation from beginning to end.

Joseph Hawley

One of my first assignments involved our Sales and Marketing organization.

The Business Partner contacted me after Cyber Operations informed the business that several servers were non-compliant because they lacked required security tools. Cybersecurity wanted to shut them down.

The problem was that these servers were older and couldn't support the required tooling. They also contained important business data, so shutting them down would have had a significant operational impact.

From cybersecurity's perspective, the concern was legitimate. The servers presented risk.

However, there wasn't an immediate threat that required shutting them down that day.

Typically, we have two options in situations like this.

Either we modernize the systems so they meet our security standards, or we isolate them from the rest of the network using a dedicated VLAN.

In this case, we isolated the servers and worked with the business on a long-term replacement plan.

What bothered me wasn't the final decision. It was how we got there.

There was no reason for Cyber Operations to contact the Director of Marketing directly and create unnecessary concern.

Instead, cybersecurity should have first discussed the situation internally, identified the available options, and then met with the Business Partner and business stakeholders to agree on a path forward.

Had we done that from the beginning, I don't believe the issue would have escalated to senior business or cybersecurity leadership.

Cybersecurity was right that action needed to be taken.

The business was also right that the situation didn't require immediate disruption.

When we handle situations like that well, people are much more willing to work with cybersecurity during future incidents that truly require urgent action.

Interviewer

If you had been involved from the beginning, what would you have done differently?

Joseph Hawley

I would have first met with Cyber Operations to fully understand the situation and the available options.

That likely would have resulted in two possible paths.

Either the servers would be upgraded or replaced, or they would be isolated on a VLAN until replacement could occur.

Once we understood those options, we would have brought together Cyber Operations, the Business Partner, and the business stakeholders to review them and agree on a path forward.

It might have required multiple meetings, but I believe we would have quickly aligned on isolating the servers in the short term while evaluating longer-term replacement options.

Joseph Hawley
