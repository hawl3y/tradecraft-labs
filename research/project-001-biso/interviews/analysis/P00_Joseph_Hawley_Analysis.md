Case Studies Discussed

Legacy Marketing Servers

Cybersecurity identified legacy marketing servers that lacked required security tooling and initially sought to shut them down. The business relied on the servers for critical operations and viewed the proposed action as unnecessarily disruptive. A Cybersecurity Partner facilitated discussions between the business and Cyber Operations, resulting in the servers being isolated on a dedicated VLAN while the business developed a long-term replacement plan.

Enterprise Cyber Incident

During a significant cybersecurity incident, systems were restored over time while portions of the business reverted to manual processes. Although the disruption was substantial, transparent communication before, during, and after the incident strengthened trust between cybersecurity and the business.

Just-in-Time Administrative Access

Cybersecurity transitioned users from permanent administrative rights to Just-in-Time administrative access. The change generated resistance and requests for exceptions. The Cybersecurity Partner helped explain the rationale, gather business concerns, and facilitate discussions between the business and cybersecurity leadership.

Evidence Supporting Thesis
The participant consistently described the role as facilitating organizational decision-making rather than making decisions.
The participant emphasized governance processes over technical implementation.
Influence was exercised through relationships and facilitation rather than formal authority.
Success was measured by how cybersecurity and business reached decisions together.
Trust enabled future collaboration even when cybersecurity decisions could not be changed.
The Cybersecurity Partner helped ensure business impacts were understood before decisions were implemented.
Evidence Challenging Thesis
Communication remained a significant responsibility of the role.
During urgent cybersecurity events, the role shifted from facilitating decisions to preparing and supporting the business through implementation.
The participant continued to describe the role as both "the face of cybersecurity" and "the voice of the business," suggesting communication remains an important mechanism of value creation.

New Themes
Governance process quality may matter independently of the technical outcome.
Stakeholder preparation emerged as a recurring responsibility.
Long-term relationship management influences future cybersecurity decisions.
Reducing unnecessary escalation may represent a distinct source of value.
The Cybersecurity Partner serves as an organizational coordination point, connecting business stakeholders with the appropriate cybersecurity teams at the appropriate time.

Researcher's Notes

The participant initially described the role using common practitioner language such as "bridge" and "liaison." As the interview progressed, however, the discussion shifted away from communication and toward governance processes, stakeholder sequencing, and decision facilitation.

Trust was not described as agreement with cybersecurity decisions. Instead, trust was framed as confidence that cybersecurity would engage stakeholders early, explain the rationale behind decisions, and support the business throughout implementation.

One unexpected observation was the importance placed on stakeholder preparation. The participant repeatedly emphasized that how cybersecurity decisions are introduced and implemented influences future collaboration, even when the technical decision itself cannot be changed.

The interview also reinforced the decision to revise the interview guide. Asking participants to describe real situations produced richer discussion around governance than directly asking about governance or trust.
